Chapter 3

The Office

Chapter 3.

Andy bounded down the stairs, two at a time.

He found his mother in the kitchen, methodically washing dishes that were already clean.

"Hey." He leaned against the doorframe. "Ready to tackle the office?"

Debby placed the towel on the counter and turned to face him. "Yes, thank you."

Andy could see the sadness in her eyes, the way they were slightly red and puffy. He felt a pang of guilt in his chest.

"I'm sorry, Mom," he said, his voice barely above a whisper. "I know this is hard for you."

She took a deep breath and pointed at a stack of folded up cardboard boxes.

"Can you help me carry these upstairs?"

"Sure!" said Andy, pushing himself off the doorframe.

Debby picked up half and gave them to Andy. Then she picked up the other half.

"Okay, let's go," she said, taking another deep breath.

They both went upstairs in silence and crossed the hallway to the office.

Debby went in first; Andy followed her.

A few dust motes danced in the afternoon sunlight streaming through the windows.

Everything remained exactly as his father had left it - the desk with its organized chaos of papers,
the whiteboard covered in equations, the family photo from their trip to Yellowstone three years ago.

His mother assembled an empty cardboard box. "Start with the bookshelf? We can donate these to the university library."
Andy nodded, pulling out a stack of technical journals. He flipped through them before placing them carefully in a cardboard box.

Memories of his father reading them downstairs in his armchair flooded back and he felt a tear welling in one eye.
He wiped at it quickly, hoping his mother hadn't noticed, but she had.
"Oh Andy," she said, dropping her box and rushing over to him, embracing him in a Mama Bear hug.
He wanted to protest, but it honestly felt too good, so he just hugged her back.

After a moment, she pulled away, her eyes glistening. "How about a good movie and some ice cream tonight. Just you and me?"

"Sounds good," said Andy, his voice thick with emotion.

She smiled. "Let's get back to work." She picked her box back up and continued to place books in it.

Andy started to wander around the room, stuffing random items in his box.

"What about stuff we want to keep?" he asked, holding up a small metal sculpture of a horse.

"Are there things you want in your room?" she asked.

"Maybe," he said, setting it back down.

"How about you pack a box with stuff you want to keep?" she suggested.

Andy nodded. "Okay."

He wandered around a bit more, when his eyes fell over to the closet. His curiosity piqued and he walked closer.

"What's in there?" he asked, pulling the door open.

"More papers. Your father never agreed to throw them out," she said, not looking up.

And indeed the closet was filled with boxes, stacked haphazardly. He started to move them around, looking for anything of interest.

Then his heart stopped, a startled yelp escaping his lips. "Holy shit!" he exclaimed jumping backwards,
while looking into the face of a young woman.

His mother turned around, books in both hands, ready to rush to her son's aid at any moment. "Andy?"

"Holy cow!" he exclaimed again.

"What's the matter, Andy?" she asked with concern in her voice, placing books into the box with disregard.
She started to move in Andy's direction.

Andy had to think quick on his feet. "Spider! A big one, behind the boxes."
He pointed at some of the boxes in front of him.

"How big?" she asked, her voice a bit shaky.
"It's huge!" He stretched his hands wide.

His mother stopped in her tracks. "Oh God," she said, visibly distressed, her breath already unsteady.
Spiders weren’t just unpleasant—they could trigger panic attacks for her. Without another word, she hurried for the door.

"Don't worry, Mom. I'll take care of it." Andy said, already feeling sorry for lying to her.

"Could you maybe bring me a plastic cup and a small piece of cardboard as cover?" he asked aloud.

Andy hoped that finding a small piece of cardboard would keep her busy for a while.

"Okay. No problem," she said, pulling the door open.
She left the room, but Andy waited until he heard her footsteps on the stairs.

He rushed back into the closet and moved a couple of boxes out of the way.
There she was again, sitting propped up against the wall. Her eyes were closed, her head tilted to the side.
She looked real - too real. "Oh Dad, really?" he muttered under his breath.

Andy needed to get her out of there before his mother came back.
He quickly grabbed her under her arms and pulled her out of the closet.
She was much heavier than her slim frame suggested, but there was no time to dwell on it.
He had to get her out of the room now.
Dragging her across the room, he moved quickly towards the open door.
Andy struggled to maintain his grip as he half-carried, half-dragged her across the hall and to his bedroom.

He heard his mother coming back to the stairs. He blew a strand of hair out of his face and parked the female body in front of him,
keeping one hand on her and opening the door to his room with the other.

"Everything okay up there?" his mother called from downstairs.
"Splendid!" He managed to keep his voice steady. "Just moving some boxes!"

Andy quickly pulled the lifeless body into his room and with another moment of struggle, he managed to get her into his bed.
He pulled his blanket over her just two seconds before his mother popped her head into the room.

"Everything okay?" she asked, concern in her voice.
"Yeah, just looking for a space for the horse," he said.
"Oh, so you wanna keep it?" she asked.
"Yeah, I think so," he said, trying to sound as casual as possible.

"He always liked that statue," she said, half smiling at him.
She raised her hands, one holding a cup, the other a piece of cardboard.
"What about the spider?"
A tinge of adrenaline shot through his body.
"Oh right, yeah let's get rid of it," he said, taking the cup and the cardboard from her.
"You wanna take a look at it?"
She shook her head, "No, thank you. Just set it free, preferably somewhere far, far away."
