Chapter 23

The Visit

Chapter 23.

Andy lay on his bed, one arm draped over his eyes, while The Smiths played softly through his speakers.

"How Soon Is Now?" seemed particularly fitting - he'd been cycling through every sad song he could find for the past three days.

His phone buzzed occasionally with messages he didn't bother to check. The news had finally moved on to other stories, but his social media remained a minefield of opinions and theories about Gia.

Everyone seemed to have something to say about the "Robot Girl Incident" or the singularity, or the ethics of AI, or the dangers of technology.

Andy had stopped reading the comments after the first day.

The curtains remained drawn, creating a cave-like atmosphere that matched his mood perfectly.

Empty energy drink cans littered his desk, testament to his disturbed sleep patterns.

His mother had tried to coax him out several times, but eventually resigned herself to leaving plates of food outside his door.

The gentle melody of "Asleep" had just started when his phone rang - his mother's ringtone cutting through the melancholy music.

"Yeah?" he answered, his voice rough from disuse.

"Andy, honey, there's someone here to see you." His mother's voice carried an odd note.

"Tell them I'm not feeling well."

"It's someone from your father's work. He says it's important."

Andy sat up slowly, his head spinning slightly from the sudden movement. "Dad's work?"

"Yes. He says he knew your father."

Andy sighed, running a hand through his unwashed hair. "Fine. I'll be down in a minute."

He pulled on a clean-ish t-shirt and tried to make himself somewhat presentable. The mirror showed dark circles under his eyes, but there wasn't much he could do about those.

The man waiting in their living room wore khakis and a blue button-down shirt with the Apex logo. He looked to be in his mid-fifties, with salt-and-pepper hair and kind eyes behind wire-rimmed glasses.

"Andy," the man stood, extending his hand. "I'm Dr. James Clark. I worked with your father in the robotics division."

Andy shook his hand half-heartedly and slumped into a chair. Dr. Clark followed suit, sitting across from him.

His mother brought in a tray of tea and cookies, then sat herself at the table between them.

"I heard about what happened at your school." Dr. Clark's voice was gentle. "It must have been terrible."

"I don't know where she is. She fell out of my car." Andy's voice was flat. He had repeated that lie too many times.

Dr. Clark smiled. "Oh, that's too bad. I was hoping you could help me with something."

Andy raised an eyebrow. "Yeah? What's that?"

Dr. Clark shrugged. "Hm, did you know that these androids have a memory chip? It's a small piece of hardware that stores all their experiences."

Andy felt a chill run down his spine.
He finally looked at Dr. Clark properly, his eyes narrowing.

"What are you saying?"

"I don't know. Say you would magically find her. And you would retrieve her memory chip. That chip could be inserted into a new android."

Andy's heart skipped a beat. "You're saying you could bring her back?"

Dr. Clark shrugged. "I'm not saying anything. I'm just saying that it's possible."
But his smile said otherwise.

Andy's mind raced. Could it be true?

"But I don't have another android."

He looked at his mother, who was watching the exchange with too big of a smile.

"Oh, yeah, that's a pity of course." He nodded his head.

"But just to be sure, what are the chances if you had to find that memory chip?"

Andy wasn't sure if he was being tricked. But the man seemed truly kind and gentle.

Dr. Clark leaned back in his chair, his eyes meeting Andy's. "Your father was a great man, Andy.
He knew he was sacrificing his time with your mother and especially with you, because he was working on a once in a lifetime project. He was working on a project that would change the world more than anything else before it. And he was so close to finishing it."

Andy swallowed hard. Tears started to form again. He blushed.
He averted his eyes, pouring some sugar into his tea.
He stirred it quickly, eager to calm his sore throat with the hot liquid.

"He always talked to Gia about you. He was so proud of you. He loved you so much."
Andy eyes welled up. Debby put her hand on his.

"I was surprised to hear your story, GX-7 models don't usually trust someone so quickly. Your dad really must have made an impression on her with his stories about you."

Andy swallowed. "I'm sorry to ask, but I am going through a lot right now. Why are you here, Mr. Clark?"

Dr. Clark leaned forward, his eyes meeting Andy's. "There was a time in my life, I'm not proud of Andy. I made mistakes. Costly mistakes because of my own arrogance, lies and ignorance. I should have been fired faster than a bullet from a 50 BMG. But your father saw something in me, he gave me a second chance, when I truly, truly didn't deserve one. He covered the losses I incurred. He took partial blame for my mistakes. In short, he saved me and my career. I've waited for a chance to repay this debt ever since. And now the time has come."

Andy and Debby looked at each other. Both of their eyes were wide with surprise.

Dr. Clark cleared his throat. "So, let me ask you again. I'm risking a lot here and I need to know for sure. What are the chances if you had to find that memory chip?"

"100%," Andy said, his voice barely above a whisper.

Dr. Clark had hoped to hear that answer. "That's good to know." He smiled.

He pulled a small envelope out of his pocket and pushed it over to Andy.

"What's this?" Andy asked, picking it up.

"This is a piece of paper with instructions on how to find and retrieve the memory chip. It should be a piece of cake with skills of your caliber. But keep in mind, you are being observed 24/7. Not only by police but also by Apex. So you will have to be very careful."

Andy nodded, thinking about all of the cars and vans waiting outside. "I understand."

Dr. Clark nodded warmly. "Officially I am here to persuade you to give up Gia's body, so if you could do that - after - you retrieve the memory chip, that would be splendid. On behalf of Apex Automation, I can tell you that we will drop any legal action against you, should we get her body back."

Debby nodded eagerly. "That's a good deal, Andy," she said, having hidden all of the paperwork from Andy about that until now.

Andy wasn't really ready to give her body to anybody, but this man had an eerily trustworthy presence about him.

Andy felt it would be a good idea to follow his advice. He didn't know why, but the feeling was very strong.

"What if the memory chip is damaged?" Andy asked, his heart pounding.

"This thing is more protected than the black box of a Boeing 747."

Andy nodded, his mind racing. "What do I do once I have it?"

Dr. Clark stood up, grabbing his coat. "You keep it safe."

"I keep it safe?" Andy repeated, his voice unsure.

Dr. Clark nodded. "You keep it safe."

Dr. Clark turned to his mother and shook her hand. "Thank you very much for your kind hospitality, Mrs. Thompson. It was very nice to meet you, even though it was under these circumstances. He talked about you endlessly."

Debby shook his hand, "Thank you for coming, Dr. Clark." Then spontaneously she pulled him in and gave him a hug.

Dr. Clark didn't know what to do at first, but then he hugged her back.

They let go of each other and he went for the door. "Goodbye Andy."

Then at the last moment he turned back around.

"Your father was very proud of his inventions, I think you can imagine, but not a second went by where he wasn't even more proud of you."

The tears Andy had been holding back spilled over he buried his face in his hands.

This was way too much for him. All the emotions he had been holding back for the past weeks came crashing down on him. He tried to find the tissue in his pocket. He didn't care that it was used already.

"Ma'am, Andy," Dr. Clark nodded and closed the door behind him.
