Chapter 24

The Kiss

Chapter 24.

Six months later.

Andy's fingers danced across the keyboard, muscle memory taking over as he guided his hero through the lush forest in the Radiant side of the battlefield. The familiar ping of abilities and items provided a welcome distraction from the emptiness of the past weeks. His room had become a fortress of solitude, soda cans and empty chip bags his only companions.

"Push top," he commanded through his headset. "Let's get megas."

His teammates' voices crackled through his headphones, a chorus of agreement and strategy. This was simple. This made sense. Here, he didn't have to think about broken androids or betrayed friendships or media circuses.

The afternoon sun tried to pierce his drawn curtains, creating a dim haze in his room.
His mother had stopped trying to get him to open them days ago.

"Andy?" His mother's voice barely registered through his headset.

"Can it wait, Mom?" he called back automatically, fingers flying across the keyboard. "We're winning!"

"Andy, there's a package for you." Something in her tone made him pause.

"Can it wait five minutes?"

"It's from a Mr. Clark Kent." Her voice trembled slightly. "And it's pretty heavy."

Andy's hand froze over his mouse. "What? Superman?"

"There's a package outside. Or rather a wooden crate. And it's heavy," his mother called from downstairs.

The sound of the enemy buildings crumbling barely registered as he ripped off his headset.

He took the stairs three at a time, nearly colliding with the wall at the bottom.

Through the front window, he could see a delivery truck backed into their driveway, and a man was wrestling with what looked like a small coffin-sized crate.

His heart hammered against his ribs as he threw open the front door.

The delivery men looked up, startled by his sudden appearance.

"Andrew Thompson?" one asked, consulting his clipboard.

Andy nodded, not trusting his voice.

"Sign here, please."

His signature was barely legible; his hand shaking too much to form proper letters.
But at least he could finally use his right hand again.

The man maneuvered the crate through their front door and, following Andy's gestures, to the side of the living room. Debby had already cleared a space for it and laid down a few pages of newspaper to avoid scratches.

"Thanks," Andy shouted, while the driver exited through the door with a wave of his hand.

Andy attacked the crate with a screwdriver from the garage.

His mother hovered in the doorway, wringing her hands.

"Slow down, Andy. You'll hurt yourself."

"I'm fine, Mom." The side panel came loose, revealing what looked like a shipping container made of brushed steel. A number lock was set into the front panel.

Andy's fingers trembled as he input the number from the letter Dr. Clark had given him three months ago.
The number was written by hand at the end of the page, without any context or explanation.
But he'd never forgotten it. 7247.

The container hissed, pressurized air escaping as the front panel slowly opened.

There, surrounded by protective foam, lay a young woman. Her knees folded uncomfortably close to her chin.

Her eyes were shut, her face was peaceful.

He carefully pulled the body out of the container, cradling her in his arms.

His mother quickly came over to help. "Oh my God, Andy," she said softly, trying to stretch the girl's legs.

Andy turned the body over, revealing a small panel at the base of her skull.
He pulled on a small lever and the panel popped open, revealing a small opening.

"I'll go get it," he said, his voice shaking.

He ran back up to his room, his mother shaking her head in disbelief.

Yanking open his desk drawer, his fingers fumbled for the small black chip he'd kept wrapped in a soft cloth.

He ran back down, but now a bit more careful, his heart pounding in his chest.

Kneeling down beside the lifeless body, he carefully inserted the chip into the slot at the base of her skull.

He closed the panel. Nothing happened. He looked inside the container and found a small power converter.

Andy plugged the power cable into the wall socket, next to the couch and the plug into the familiar socket at the hip of the android.

For a moment, nothing happened. Then her eyelids fluttered, and Andy's breath caught in his throat.

Her eyes opened, focusing immediately on his face. A smile spread across her features - that same smile that had first captured his heart.

"Andy," she said softly, "I'm so happy to see you. What happened?"

Tears blurred his vision as he stepped closer.

"I'll tell you everything," his voice cracked. "But first, may I kiss you?"

Her smile widened. "Of course, Andy." But then, her gaze fell on Debby. She looked back at Andy with concern.

"It's okay," he said, his voice barely a whisper. "She knows everything."

"I don't know," she whispered back. "Better to ask her first."

Andy cried and laughed at the same time. He shook his head. "Mom, may I kiss Gia?"

"Jesus Christ, Andy, what are you waiting for?" his mother said, her voice breaking.

She held her hands over her mouth, tears streaming down her face.

Andy pulled Gia into his arms, pressing his lips against hers.

She felt exactly the same - warm, soft, real.

In that moment, all the pain and fear of the past weeks melted away.

Behind them, Andy's mother quietly went upstairs, leaving them alone.

There would be time later for a million questions, for dealing with a world that now knew Gia's secret.

But for now, in this moment, everything was perfect.

"I love you, Gia," he said, his voice breaking.

"I love you too, Andy," she whispered back.
