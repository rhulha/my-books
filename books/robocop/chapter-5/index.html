Ellen

Chapter 5 - Ellen.

The door opened and a woman appeared — her beauty dulled by swollen eyes and dark circles telling of a sleepless night.

Her gaze fell on Alex and she had to steady herself.

She held on to the door, making sure not to lose her balance.

She had seen him once before, while he was still in a coma.

So this wasn't a complete surprise, but with Alex now being awake it hit differently.

"Alex?"

"Hey babe." Alex attempted a smile, not sure if that muscle was even, what did they call it? Calibrated?

She rushed to her husband and threw her arms around his neck.

"Oh Alex." She started to kiss his cheek over and over. "I can't believe you are alive."

"I heard... I have you... to thank for that."

"You are the greatest fighter I have ever seen."

"They told me it was pretty tough."

Ellen eyes welled up again. "You are my greatest hero. You hear me! You are my hero."

"But I didn't... do anything."

"You are a fighter Alex, you hear me! The doctors told me the chance of your survival was minuscule. But you fought. You fought for us."

"I can't remember anything."

Ellen pulled back a little to look at him. "It's okay. You don't have to remember right now. Just focus on getting better."

"They told me... all that's left... is my brain."

Ellen nodded, fighting back tears. "That's all that matters to me."

Alex looked at her. "You are the greatest... wife on earth... you know that?"

Ellen closed her eyes, trying to hold back the flood of emotions, but a few tears escaped.

"Don't worry, Alex. We'll get through this."

"I want to look... at myself," said Alex after a moment.

Ellen hesitated for a moment, then nodded.

She picked up a mirror from the bedside table and took a step back.

Then she held it up in front of Alex, so he could see his reflection.

What Alex saw next, reminded him of science fiction movies.

OCP's engineers had reconstructed his face with DermaTec — a medical-grade silicone elastomer, the company's own formulation, used in high-end facial reconstruction. They'd matched his skin tone. Rebuilt the slight asymmetry of his nose, the familiar set of his jaw, the soft crease at the corner of his mouth. Under the fluorescent hospital lights, with Ellen standing three feet away, it could pass. Almost.

His head rested on a gleaming, metallic torso. It was as though someone had taken his face and grafted it onto a hulking robotic frame.

His neck was now encased in a lattice of black cables and chrome-like plates.

His shoulders were broad, unnaturally so, extending into powerful, gunmetal arms that glistened under the fluorescent hospital lights.

Instead of skin, smooth metal reflected his surroundings.

The plating fit together with precision, no gaps or visible seams, forming a solid protective shell, with panels subtly contoured to mimic the broad shape of a muscular torso.

Alex's gaze followed the mirrored reflection, taking in the imposing structure of his shoulders and arms.

The limbs were massive yet proportionate, their polished metal glinting under the lights.

His arms ended in mechanical black hands, each finger articulated with delicate precision.

His limbs, if they could even be called that, were unnervingly perfect in proportion yet entirely inhuman.

His head sat upon a monstrous body that could never feel the sand between his toes again and never hold Ellen the way he used to.

He was trapped in a shell of steel, with only his mind tethered to the human life he once knew.

"I'm sorry, Ellen."

"It's not your fault."

"I'm not... me anymore."

Ellen shook her head. "Yes, you are. You are still Alex. The most important part of you is still here."

Ellen tried to push Alex on his shoulder, but it was like hitting a steel beam - unyielding, cold.

Ellen quickly pulled her hand back. Shocked by her own reaction, she looked at Alex.

"You better start... getting used to that."

Ellen's eyes welled up. "I'm sorry, I didn't mean…"

"Don't. It's okay. We will have to get used to a lot of new things."

She took his cold, black hand. "Yes. We will."
