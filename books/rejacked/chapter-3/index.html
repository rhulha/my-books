Chapter 3

The Amish Girl

Chater 3 - The Amish Girl.

Lizzy slowly stirred awake as the first rays of sunlight hit her lids, savoring the warmth for just a few moments longer before her eyes flew open with the realization of what day it was. Jolting awake, Lizzy flung back the covers and clambered to kneel for her morning prayers.

“Thank you, Father, for giving me my life, my family, and this day!”

The girl’s voice rose almost to a squeal by the time she reached the end of her thanks and then she was down the stairs in a flash.

“Mama!” Lizzy sang as she performed her classic swing around the end of the banister.

Blowing a silver-streaked, loose brown wisp back towards her bonnet, a bustling woman dried off her dishes before setting them on the wooden table next to a basket of fresh green apples.

Lizzy bounced and wrapped her mother in a hug that threatened the dishes’ safety, kissing her cheek.

“Good morning, mama,” Lizzy said, swiping an apple with twinkling eyes despite the warning look she received.

“Oh, Lord, give me strength . . .” Lizzy’s mother shook her head. “Good morning, my bright, energetic, mischievous daughter. Have you finished your pray-”

“Yes! I’m all done.”

“Don’t interrupt, child” Lizzy’s mother childed gently, but her brow remained soft as she looked towards her daughter. A small smile tugged at the corner of her mouth even while her eyes began to brim. Luckily, her little one was too caught up in the excitement of the morning to notice before she had the chance to blink back her tears.

Lizzy took a bite, delighting in the crisp crunch and tart flavor as she turned towards the kitchen window. She gazed dreamily over the field where the sun peeked over the horizon, her mind swirling with anticipation and questions. Before Lizzy knew it, she was bouncing in place, repeatedly squeaking the floorboards beneath her feet.

“Oh, you either put some of that energy to work helping me in the kitchen or take it outside!” her mother pleaded.
With a giggle and a wrinkle of her nose, Lizzy skipped outside to say the rest of her good-mornings and dodge any extra chores.

Lizzy quickly located her father between the house and the barn with their buggy. Their two horses stood idly nearby, their leads fixed to the nearest fencepost, grazing while they waited. The chestnut nickered and pawed lightly at the ground while she approached, earning a gentle shush from her father.

“Good morning, Papa!” Lizzy called out, her eyes eagerly focused on where he was tinkering with the carriage. Narrowing her eyes at the horses as they eyed her apple, she crouched slightly and waited for her father to stand to his full height. The instant he turned around, she flung herself into his arms with a bear hug.

“Mercy, mercy–careful there, Lizzy!” he cried, the lines around his eyes crinkling slightly, “I’m not as young as I once was, you know! You must be more careful with me than that.”

Despite her father’s words, Lizzy soon felt his strong arms lift her off the ground in a hug of his own, spinning her around once before setting her back on her feet.

“Sorry. I just love you a lot, Papa. May I ask what you are doing?”

“Well, I love you too, my dear,” came the reply as the seasoned farmer dusted off his hands, “And I’m fiddling with this wagon shaft. I’ll start working on the new one tomorrow, but we need the wagon to work properly today either way. Do you mind helping a frail, old man find a quick solution?”

Lizzy giggled once again and nodded, breaking the half-eaten remains of her apple in two and giving one piece to each horse.

“What can I do to help?” she asked, wiping her sticky fingers on her frock–although a quick glance at the thin beam of wood that normally connected the buggy and harness mounts revealed the problem: A split had started at one end of the shaft that would only get worse on the long ride ahead of them.

“Please go to the barn and fetch me that spare leather strap.”

“Yes, Papa!” Lizzy replied brightly, a purposeful spring in her step while she made her way to the barn.

“Just behind the door and to the left!” her father called after her.

But Lizzy already knew. In a matter of moments she sauntered back, proudly displaying the leather strap as requested. Her father took it with a smile, holding the split shaft together around the joint with his calloused hands, biting down firmly on one end of the taut leather as he wrapped it.

Lizzy watched his every move with bright eyes–just in case she’d need to do it herself someday.

Right as he finished expertly reinforcing the shaft and securing his work with an Amish farmer’s knot, his wife called out:

“Abraham! Lizzy! Breakfast time!”

And, just like that, Lizzy darted off. She struggled to keep from bouncing across the floorboards once more as her stomach growled for the spread that her mother seemed to have arranged with lightning speed. The scent of freshly-cooked eggs and pot pie with steaming beef gravy and Friendship Bread immediately made Lizzy forget all about the snack she’d shared with the horses.

“Thank you, Esther,” Lizzy heard her father say from the doorway, hanging his hat before taking a seat, “This looks wonderful, as always.”

Lizzy crept her hand forward to steal a flake of the hot, buttery pie crust–her favorite. However, her mother slapped her fingers away gently but firmly before she could get far.

“I’m happy to do it, of course,” Esther said primly, motioning for Lizzy to sit in her place at the table and wait. She caught her husband’s eye and shook her head slowly, smiling at how much of his spirit she saw in their daughter. The farmer returned the smile with a sigh, wordlessly stroking his beard with contentment as he got comfortable.

Esther finished handing out forks and then sat with her family, clasping hands in a circle for prayer. Abraham cleared his throat and began:

“Oh Lord, we thank you for this food and for the opportunity to enjoy it together as a family. May we use the strength it gives us only for your service. Amen.”

Esther raised her head to give Lizzy a warning eye and add emphasis to his final point as they concluded their Grace.

“And don’t you forget it, daughter! I don’t want you going wild out there. Your rumspringa is meant to help you broaden your perspective, not dive into dangerous vices with wild abandon. Don’t do anything you’d be ashamed to tell me about later!”

Lizzy nodded in acknowledgement, even though her eyes were fixed on the serving spoon as it delivered a hefty helping of pot pie to her father’s plate. She gripped her fork firmly as the next serving made its way onto her own plate, beaming thanks towards her mother before digging in.

“I understand, Mama. I don’t want to do anything bad, I just . . . I want to see what everything is like! Can you tell me more about the city? Just one more time?”

Esther winced as her daughter shoveled in a mouthful of pot pie before completing her question.

“Eating and speaking should remain two separate tasks, child,” she admonished.

“I’m sorry,” Lizzy said after swallowing, trying her hardest to offer a genuine apology despite her intense curiosity, “But please, won’t you tell me? What’s it like there?”

“Very different from when I was there myself, I imagine,” Esther remarked, her brow furrowing. She paused for a moment after dishing out her own breakfast, sharing a questioning look with her husband as she placed the spoon carefully back in its place.

Lizzy closed her eyes as the pie crust seemed to melt in her mouth the instant it met her tongue, oblivious to her father’s nod of affirmation.

“Lizzy,” Esther began, pausing until her daughter finished chewing to make sure she was really paying attention, “As I’m sure you’ve heard from other members of our community, there have always been stark differences between the tools we use and those of the outside world . . . But those differences have grown very much over the past decade or two. There have been some remarkable inventions since I ventured to the city on my own rumspringa.”

Lizzy temporarily pushed aside her half-eaten helping of pot pie to dig into her eggs, listening and wondering what her mother was getting at. After a brief moment’s pause, Abraham set his fork down to clear his throat quietly before contributing to the conversation.

“I’ve been told that, with the invention of Virtual Reality, there have come all manner of games you cannot even imagine. Much more advanced and convincing than the video games that your mother and I tried when we came of age.”

Esther dipped her head in agreement and launched into her own explanation:

“Virtual Reality is apparently very intense–so intense that it can even feel real, like whatever you’re doing is actually happening. So, please: From a moral standpoint, be careful! I must admit that I’m not sure where our Lord stands on the actions you carry out in games, but I’d urge you to avoid doing anything in Virtual Reality that you wouldn’t do in real life. God knows what’s in your heart even if your actions don’t have real-life consequences.”

Lizzy digested her parents’ words as she returned to her pot pie–the last item on her plate. For a moment she struggled with how to phrase her next question, not wanting to imply a level of rebellion that might get her in trouble, but unable to go without asking.

“. . . If I ever did want to try something risky, wouldn’t it be better to do it in Virtual Reality than in real life?”

Lizzy’s mother shook her head and exhaled heavily.

“Now look here, daughter: That is a dangerous question to play around with. I cannot condone you doing anything risky or immoral anywhere, no matter whether it supposedly counts in real life or not. That means no violence or other wicked acts–and absolutely no . . . VR sex!”

Abraham coughed abruptly and washed his bread down with a bit of water. Lizzy’s eyes traveled between her parents’ faces but found no clarity amidst the awkward silence.

“What’s VR sex?” Lizzy finally asked. Luckily for Abraham, whose eyes fixed firmly on his plate, Esther was all too happy to respond:

“You’ll find out. And when you do, don’t do it!”
