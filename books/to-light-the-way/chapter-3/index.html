Chapter 3

The wind started before sunrise

Day 3

The wind started before sunrise. It came in pulses, like the planet was breathing through clenched teeth. Fine dust crept through the seams of the airlock, coating the floor in a thin red film. I wiped it away with my sleeve, knowing it would be back by nightfall.

Ann spoke today.

I found her by the viewport, knees drawn up, arms wrapped tight around her chest. I sat beside her and handed her a ration bar. She took it without a word. We chewed in silence.

Then, without turning to me, she said, “Was it my fault?”

Her voice cracked halfway through the sentence. Rough. Unused. Like she’d been holding it in for days and it tore something on the way out.

I told her no. I told her it was the storm, or the landing vector, or maybe just bad luck. I didn’t know. I still don’t. But I said it wasn’t her fault, and I said it like I meant it.

She looked at me. First time since the crash. Her eyes were rimmed with red, skin gray from stress and poor sleep. But she was still in there—Ann, the woman who once argued for two hours about the pH balance of alien soil. The botanist who used to hum while she worked, who packed mint leaves in her lab kit like they were sacred relics.

I told her we were alive. That energy was stable. The reactor had rerouted through the secondary grid, and the core was holding steady. I even showed her the readings, hoping numbers would be more convincing than words.

Then I told her the comms were down.

She didn’t say anything, just stared at the floor. I walked her through the damage—antenna gone, transmitter offline, software unresponsive. I told her I was working on it. That I had a plan. That if I could scavenge enough from the auxiliary systems, maybe I could rebuild a short-range link. Maybe we could reach Voyager when it gets close enough.

She nodded. Not like she believed me, more like she wanted to.

We went through the inventory together. Food’s not an issue. Not with just the two of us. I didn’t say it out loud, but we both knew why. The storage was packed for four.

She found a bag of freeze-dried strawberries and just held it in her hands. I tried to keep talking—to explain the next steps, the checklist, the repair schedule—but my voice trailed off when I saw her eyes welling up again. She didn’t cry, not really. Just sat there, breathing hard, eyes shining in the dim light.

I looked away.

Told her we’d be okay. That we’d make it to the landing site. That we’d build the outpost like we were meant to. That we’d be ready when Voyager arrived. I even smiled.

She smiled back. It broke something in me.

After she went to rest, I came back to the console. Logged this entry. Tried to sound like a man with a plan.

But the truth is, I don’t have one.

I’m making it up one wire at a time.
