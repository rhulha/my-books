Chapter 2

the main energy systems

Ok, so I managed to get the main energy systems back online.
Ann has calmed down. She is eating and drinking, so that's good news I guess.
She still doesn't talk, but at least she is not screaming anymore.
I tried to send a message to Voyager, but comms are down.

The comm station looked intact at first—no visible cracks, no scorch marks—but the diagnostics told another story. The antenna array had sheared off in the crash. A clean break, like someone had taken a knife to an artery. No uplink. No downlink. I opened the panel anyway. Wires had snapped loose, some melted at the joints. One capacitor had burst, blackening the circuit board. I tried to reroute power through the backup transmitter, but the software refused to initialize. No handshake, no signal, just static.

I spent hours rerunning the same sequence, hoping for something different. I even climbed up the hull to look for the emergency beacon. Gone. Probably vaporized when the engine compartment exploded. My fingers bled from a slip on the metal edge. I wrapped the cut with tape and kept working.

By late afternoon, the sun dipped low—red and swollen through the dust-choked sky. I gave up. Not forever. Just for that day. My head throbbed from the low pressure and dehydration. I needed rest. Or food. Or both.

Inside the cabin, Ann still sat on the floor beside the supply crate. She stared at the wall, knees drawn to her chest. Her eyes followed me when I moved, but she didn’t speak. Not a word since the crash. But the screaming had stopped, and that was something.

I found the emergency rations and boiled a pack of protein broth on the thermal coil. The smell reminded me of hospital food. I poured some into a cup and added a few dry leaves from her personal stash—mint, I think. She'd brought it for hydroponics, but I figured she’d forgive me.

I placed the tea near her feet. She didn’t react. I sat across from her and opened my own packet. Chewed in silence. After a minute, she reached for the cup. Her hands trembled, but she drank. Slow sips. Careful. She didn’t look at me, but she finished it and set the empty cup down.

I wanted to say something—anything—but my throat felt raw, like I'd swallowed sand. So I said nothing. We just sat there. Two survivors in a dead machine, waiting for a future that might never come.

Maybe they can turn around.
But what about us? I don't want to die here alone.
