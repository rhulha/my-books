Chapter 9.

The day had been another long one at school, but Andy was finally back home again.
He had martyred his brain, trying to come up with a plan to not have to hide Gia anymore.

Right in the middle of computer science class, he had an idea.

He hugged his mother and kissed her on the cheek. "Hi Mom. How are you today?"

"I'm fine, Andy. But you are a bit more energetic than usual, no?"

"I have some news."

His mother turned to him, her eyes sparkling with curiosity.

She put the handkerchief down on the table and wiped her hands on it. "Great, can you help me with the dinner first?"

Andy nodded and went to the kitchen.

"Mom, I've been thinking about Dad's office."

He grabbed two plates and a few forks from the cupboard.

His mother paused, wooden spoon in hand. "What about it?"

"What if... what if we could help someone with that empty space?"

Andy placed plates and silverware on the dining table while his mother prepared dinner.

She turned to face him.

"There's this exchange student program at school," Andy continued. "They're looking for host families."

She raised an eyebrow and set down the spoon. "A student? In your father's office?"

"We could clean it up, make it nice." Andy held his breath.

"I don't know Andy, these things can get complicated. I don't know if I have the strength to deal with it so soon. Seeing someone unfamiliar walk in and out of that room."

His mother slowly resumed stirring the pot on the stove, still contemplating the suggestion.

Andy quickly picked up the object of his mother's concern. "They understand that these things have been issues in the past, so they added a new regrets option. If we don't like it, the student has to go, no questions asked."

His mother sighed. "I don't know, Andy. I'm not sure I'm ready for this."

"I understand, but with that new regrets option, we can't lose. We can help someone and if it doesn't work out, they'll be gone."

His mother leaned her head from side to side, "Hm, that does sound reasonable."

"So what do you say? One chance?" Andy smiled at her, hopeful.

She looked at him, her eyes softening. "Okay, one chance. But if it doesn't work out, they're gone. No questions asked."

Andy snapped his right fingers and swung his fist back gesturing "You're outta here".

Debby laughed and shook her head. "Okay, let's eat."

Andy nodded eagerly. "Thank you, Mom. You won't regret it, I promise."
