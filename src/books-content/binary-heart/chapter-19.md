Chapter 19.

Sunday dragged by like molasses; Andy checked his phone every hour, expecting some message from Tom - an apology, an explanation, even an angry tirade. But there was nothing. Just silence.

Andy spent the day discussing party etiquette with Gia.
He was impressed by how quickly she picked up on the nuances of human interaction.

"Perhaps Tom's blood-alcohol level affected his decision-making capabilities," she suggested, sitting cross-legged on Andy's bed.

"Yeah, no kidding." Andy rubbed his bruised knuckles.

Monday morning arrived with a knot in Andy's stomach.
The drive to school felt longer than usual; each stoplight made his anxiety grow.

"Your heart rate is elevated," Gia noted from the passenger seat.

Andy glanced at her. "You can tell?"

She reached over and squeezed his hand. Her touch was warm and soft.

The first three periods passed in a blur of nervous anticipation. Tom's usual seat remained empty in Chemistry. In English, Andy caught himself staring at the door every time it opened. By third period Math, the tension had started to fade, replaced by a hollow sort of disappointment.

The cafeteria buzzed with its usual lunchtime chaos when Andy and Gia found their usual table.
Andy picked at his sandwich, his appetite diminished by the morning's stress.

"Hello Gia, hello Andy." Ryan Carter's voice cut through Andy's thoughts like a knife.

Andy's head snapped up. Ryan stood at their table, hands in his letterman jacket pockets, wearing his trademark smirk.

"Hi Ryan," Gia replied pleasantly.

"Hi Ryan," Andy echoed, his voice tight.

"That was quite the party, wasn't it?" Ryan pulled out a chair and sat down uninvited.

"It was," Andy managed.

"Did you have a good time?"

"Cut the bullshit, Ryan," said Andy, his voice low.

"Oh, what did I miss?" Ryan took a fry from Andy's tray, dipped it into some ketchup and popped it into his mouth.

"You know exactly what happened, and if you're here to gloat, you can simply get lost." Andy's eyes narrowed.

Ryan's smirk widened. "Tom's a crazy guy." He twirled his finger beside his head. "Craaaaazy."

Andy swallowed hard. "He had way too much to drink."

"He had way too much to say." Ryan's eyes locked onto Andy's. "Way too much to say."

The cafeteria seemed to fade away, leaving just the three of them in a bubble of tension.

"He said some things he shouldn't have said." Ryan leaned forward. "Things that are impossible."

He paused. "Then again, they explain everything."

Andy pushed his lunch tray aside, his sandwich forgotten. "Look Ryan, Tom is a good friend of mine, but he really is crazy sometimes."

Ryan laughed, the sound sharp and humorless. "Tom is a good friend of yours?"

"Yes."

"So good, he would keep a secret for you?"

Sweat beaded on Andy's forehead. The fluorescent lights suddenly felt too bright, too hot.

He cursed the stupid grocery store, he cursed that he didn't listen to himself about this stupid party, he cursed Tom and finally he cursed himself.

Ryan reached across the table and took another fry and brought it to his mouth.

Andy pushed his plate to Ryan. "Here, take it all, Ryan. I don't give a fuck."

His eyes never left Andy's as he slowly bit into the fried potato slice.

"What used to be yours," he said softly, "will soon all be mine."
