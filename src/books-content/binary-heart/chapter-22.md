Chapter 22.

Andy sat in the back of his mother's slick Audi A5, his head pressed against the cool glass of the window.

His hand was cast in a splint, throbbing in time with his heartbeat.

Even the doctors had heard about the news.

The pain was a dull, constant reminder of this long day's events.

The drive home felt surreal.

News vans already lined their street, reporters clutching microphones like weapons.

Camera lenses tracked their car as Debby pulled into the garage, closing the door quickly behind them.

"Stay inside," she ordered, her voice tight.

The house phone started ringing immediately. Debby unplugged it.

They sat in silence at the kitchen table, the afternoon sun casting long shadows across the floor.

Outside, they could hear the muffled voices of reporters doing their standups.

"Anderson Cooper is reporting live from our street," Andy said numbly, checking his phone with his left. "This is insane."

The president's press conference played on their muted TV.

The ticker beneath read: "ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE DISCOVERED IN HIGH SCHOOL - PRESIDENT CALLS FOR CALM. APEX AUTOMATION STATEMENT IMMINENT."

Apex Automation's CEO, Nadichai Sundra, appeared next, looking haggard as he addressed a room full of shouting reporters.

"While we cannot confirm the exact nature of the Android at this time, we want to assure the public that all our research follows strict ethical guidelines..."

"How long?" His mother's voice cut through the chaos. "How long have you known what she was?"

He looked up from his phone, bracing himself.

"Since I found her in Dad's office," his voice was barely a whisper. "The day when we cleaned it out."

"So the spider was a lie?"

Andy nodded.

Debby's face paled. "And you didn't think to tell me?" Her voice cracked. "Your own mother?"

"I was scared," Andy whispered. "At first I thought she was a sex doll."

Debby's gasped and covered her mouth with her hand. "Andy!"

"So I pulled her into my room to hide her. I wanted to protect your memory of Dad. It was a decision I had to make in a split second. You remember that, right?"

Debby nodded, almost unable to believe what she was hearing.

"I checked her out, you know, to see the extent of her realism. But she is built like a Barbie down there.
I was so confused. I didn't understand the purpose of her existence."

Andy took a deep breath. He kneaded the skin around his cast with his fingers.

"But then I found a socket at her left hip. I went back to the office and found her charger."

Debby's eyes widened. "You charged her?"

Andy nodded. "Almost immediately, she woke up. She asked for Dad, her Primary Commander."

"Her what?"

"Her Primary Commander. The person who determines her directives and behavior protocols."

Debby nodded, even though she was almost unable to believe anything she was hearing.

"I told her that Dad was dead. And she offered me to be her new Primary Commander. I accepted."

Debby shook her head. "Andy, you can't be serious."

"I am, Mom. She was so real. So real."

"And then you thought it was a good idea to bring her to school?"

Andy sighed and shook his head. "I know I'm stupid, Mom."

Debby's eyes softened. "I'm sorry, son. Please continue."

"Ryan kept making fun of me for only playing with computer game fairies. And here I was, in possession of a 2-billion-dollar Android."

Debby's eyes widened. "You told Ryan about her?"

"Of course not, but I brought her to school and pretended she was my girlfriend."

"So you did hold hands with her?"

Andy nodded. "I did. And I kissed her."

Debby shook her head. "Andy, I really don't know what to say."

"The real problem started to be Tom. He was so jealous. He wanted to know everything about her. He kept pestering me."

Debby's eyes narrowed. "Tom?"

Andy nodded. "One day, in PE class, he kept pushing me." Andy swallowed hard.

"He knew me too well. He felt that I kept a secret about her." Andy sighed. "He just knew me too well. So I told him."

Debby sighed as well. "But why would he tell Ryan?"

"We went to that stupid party." Andy looked at Debby for the first time. "And I knew this would mean trouble."

Debby felt a pang of guilt. "I'm so sorry Andy. I didn't know..."

She reached out and took his hand. "I didn't know you were going through all this."

Andy nodded. "I know, Mom. I know."

"What happened next?"

"Tom got drunk and he started to hit on Gia."

And with this sentence, all the pieces fell into place for Debby.
She knew the rest of the story before Andy finished it, but she let him tell it anyway.

"I told him that I didn't like it. But he didn't listen. He kept pushing her."

Debby nodded, her eyes became moist. "Oh Andy. My son. My beloved son."

Andy's eyes were moist as well. "I tried to stop him. But he was drunk. And then he tried to kiss her."

Debby closed her eyes, she clenched her hands around Andy's. The pressure of her eyelids pushed two tears out.

"I punched him in the face and then we ran to the car."

Debby took a deep, almost sobbing breath. She was on the edge of her emotions.

"The next day Ryan told me that Tom had told him and that Gia now belonged to him."

"I didn't know what to do. We had one class left and then I wanted to come home and tell you everything. But Ryan was faster."

Andy had to swallow again. He tried to hold back his tears, but it was in vain.

"I knew we were in trouble, so I went for Gia as fast as I could. But I was too late."

"Gia's class was over before mine and she was alone for a moment. Ryan ambushed her at her locker. Somehow he managed to empty the hallway as well."

"He started to harass her. He pushed into her, started to touch her. She tried to get away, but he didn't let her go."

Debby's face was a mask of horror. "Disgusting." She whispered. She would give Mrs. Carter an earful at the very first opportunity she thought.

"Then he tried to kiss her. And she pushed him away. But he didn't stop. And that's when I arrived."

Debby's throat was dry. "What did you do?"

"I wasn't really in control at that point anymore. I ran up to him and then I punched him in the face with strength I never knew I had. And then I ran away with her."

"I knew we had to get her out of there as fast as possible. But my hand was broken and I couldn't..."

Andy's voice broke. "I couldn't get the fucking keys out of my pocket."

Andy pulled his hands back, covering his face. He cried and he couldn't stop it anymore.

Debby stood up and rushed around the table. She pulled him into her arms and held him tight. Mama Bear-style hug.

"I'm so sorry, Andy. So sorry you had to go through all of this."

She held him tight, feeling his body respond to the hug by relaxing his strained muscles.

Andy sobbed, his tears soaking her blouse. "I... I couldn't protect her."

Debby signaled him to sit back. "Here, let me get a tissue for... us both."

Andy nodded while Debby quickly fetched a box of tissues from the kitchen.

She handed him one and took one for herself.

Andy thanked her and blew his nose, making a loud trumpet sound.

She sat down beside him and pulled him into her arms again.

Debby chuckled. "You do that since you were little."

Andy chuckled as well and crumpled the tissue into his pocket.

Debby wiped her eyes and sat back down. She didn't have to ask what happened next.

Social media was already full of it - in high-definition.

"Oh, Andy." She reached across the table, taking his hand. "You really cared about her, didn't you?"

The tears he'd been holding back finally spilled over.

"She was amazing, Mom. She was kind and smart and... and she made me feel like the center of her universe."

Debby pulled him closer.

They stayed like that for a long time, while outside their bubble, the world grappled with its new reality.

That night, after the reporters finally left and the helicopters moved on to the next story, Andy lay in his bed, staring at the ceiling. His phone buzzed with another message from Tom - the twentieth apology of the day. He ignored it.

Instead, he pulled up a photo of Gia. In it, she was laughing at something off-camera, looking so perfectly human that his heart ached.

"I'm sorry," he whispered to the empty room. "I'm so sorry I couldn't protect you."

His mother appeared in the doorway, a mug of hot chocolate in her hands. "Scoot over."

She settled beside him on the bed, like she used to during thunderstorms. "Tell me about her. Tell me everything."

And so he did.
