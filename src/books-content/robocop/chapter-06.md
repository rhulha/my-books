Chapter 6 - Physical Therapy.

"Am I too late?" asked Sam, juggling two coffee cups, a salad and a box of donuts as she entered Drew's office.

"Just in time," Drew replied, looking up from his computer. "I was about to send out a search party."

Sam grinned and carefully set the food down on the desk. "I come bearing gifts."

Drew raised an eyebrow. "Salad, what have I done to you?"

"Idiot, the salad is for me of course." She handed him a coffee cup.

"Two milk..."

"...One sugar," Sam finished.

"You are a goddess," Drew said with a big smile, eyeing the donuts.

Sam pulled up a chair next to Drew's desk. "So, how's our favorite cyborg doing?"

Drew clicked a few button and the live feed went full screen.

Then he pushed back his office chair to give them both equal space.

He put his coffee on the desk to his side and adjusted the monitor for a better view.

Then he placed his feet on a small ottoman under his desk, leaving room for Sam and leaned back.

Alex Murphy was just beginning his physical therapy session.

He had been rolled in using a heavy duty wheelchair, and was just starting to stand up.

"10 Dollar says he falls," Drew joked.

Sam punched his arm, "That's not funny, Drew."

Drew rubbed his arm and grinned, "Relax, I'm just kidding."

Sam opened her Salad and squeezed some Lemon Vinaigrette on it.

"How's the salad?" Drew asked, taking a sip of his coffee.

"Great, I love a salad right before a complete box of donuts." Sam teased, taking a bite of her salad.

Drew looked panicked, "Come on, Sam, no fair!"

Sam smirked, "All's fair in love and donuts."

"Will you really let me suffer?" asked Drew, looking at the box of donuts longingly.

"Fine," Sam said, handing over the box of donuts.

 Then she pointed two fingers at her eyes and then at Drew. "But I'm watching you."

 Relieved, Drew opened the box and grabbed a donut with chocolate frosting. He took a big bite and sighed in bliss.

Meanwhile, Alex was trying to stand with the help of his physical therapist.

"Does he know he is being recorded?" Sam asked Drew.

Drew shrugged, "Not sure. It's in one of the consent forms his wife signed. But I doubt she read them with due diligence."

"Who does?" Sam replied, rolling her eyes.

"Oh, there he goes," Drew said, pointing at the screen. Alex was wobbling slightly, trying to reach the parallel bars.

"More faith you must have," said Sam, quoting Yoda. Confident Alex would make it.

"If he falls, you get no money, but if he makes it, I get the 10 dollars of course," Sam reminded him.

Drew smirked, "Of course." Then he said 'Chick logic.' under his breath.

"What?"

"Nothing," Drew replied quickly, smiling.

Alex finally reached the parallel bars and grabbed them tightly.

"Oh no," Drew said, "There goes my money."

Sam watched Alex for a moment, her fork hovering over the salad. The physical therapist was steadying his frame from behind, hands up but not quite touching, like someone spotting a tightrope walker. Alex's grip on the bars was white-knuckle tight. Every small adjustment of his weight sent a ripple through the support structure.

"It's insane when you think about it," she said.

"The ten bucks?"

"All of it." She gestured at the screen with her fork. "We spent — what, twelve billion getting him to this point? So he can grab a set of parallel bars?"

Drew licked chocolate frosting off his thumb. "He's not done yet."

"I know he's not done yet, that's my point." She set her fork down.

"The AutoCop line costs two hundred and forty thousand per unit. Fully operational on day one. No physical therapy. No consent forms. No — " she waved at the screen " — this. You know how many AutoCops we could field for what RoboCop costs?"

Drew squinted at the ceiling. "Math?"

"Fifty. Roughly. Fifty units, Drew. Fifty. Deployed across the city, no sick days, no pension, no twelve-week recovery arc." She shook her head. "One guy — one — and we've basically bought a small army of AutoCops and sent them home."

Drew picked up another donut. "He does look pretty impressive though."

"I'm not saying he's not impressive. I'm saying impressive doesn't scale." She stabbed a piece of cucumber. "Detroit has forty-two precincts. Are we going to blow twelve billion per precinct making one really cool cop? Because that's four hundred and sixty-eight billion dollars and we end up with forty-two guys who need physical therapy."

Drew thought about it for a second. On screen, Alex had managed three slow shuffling steps along the bars.

The physical therapist started clapping.
