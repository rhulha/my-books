Chapter 15: Back at the PD.

Alex would get his own reinforced patrol car soon, but today Ellen dropped him off at the precinct.

She pressed a button and the van's large side door slid open and the hydraulics moved the chair out and down to the ground.

Alex stepped out, his movements smooth and precise.

A few beat reporters couldn't believe their luck and they started to snap photos like machine gunners.

"Good luck," said Ellen, giving him a small wave before she drove off, with a knot in her stomach.

Alex felt it too, but he was determined to face it head on.

He turned and headed towards the precinct, his footsteps echoing in the quiet morning air.

The doors slid open with the familiar hiss and he stepped inside, taking in the familiar environment.

Awkward silence fell over the bullpen as he entered.

Through his enhanced vision, he could see officers freezing mid-conversation, coffee cups halting halfway to mouths, fingers pausing over keyboards.

Alex nodded to a few familiar faces, acknowledging their presence without breaking his focus.

"Welcome back, Murphy", said sergeant Warren Reed with a warm smile. "Good to have you on the force again."

"Thank you, sir." Alex's voice modulator carried his words clearly, though he noticed several officers flinch at its mechanical undertone.

"We were just about to start the morning briefing. Why don't you join us?" he added.

Alex nodded and followed the sergeant to the conference room.

A large, metal chair was set up for him at a table to the left.

He took it and sat down, feeling the weight of the stares from his colleagues.

"It's good to have you back, Murphy," said one of the officers, trying to break the ice.

"Good to be back," Alex replied, his voice calm and steady.

Reed went to the front of the room and turned around.

"Before we get started, I want to take a moment and welcome Murphy back," he said, looking around at the officers. "He's been through a lot, and we're glad to have him back on the team."

The officers nodded in agreement and all started clapping lightly.

"This is a new chapter for Murphy and for all of us and I know this team will continue to support each other, God knows we need it," Reed continued.

The room murmured in agreement.

"Well, as you all know, we have a lot of work to do."

"This fucking Nuke is a bane on our city and we need to step up our efforts to take it down," he said, his tone becoming more serious.

Reed clicked to the next slide. A map of Detroit, two clusters of red pins bleeding across it like separate wounds.

"We're working on the assumption that there are two production facilities," he said.

"Lab boys have been running analysis on seized products." Reed continued, tapping the screen.

"The batches come in two slightly different varieties.
The lab guys found two different set of trace impurities - different enough that the chemistry guys say it's likely two separate production facilities."

He clicked again. Side-by-side photos of confiscated packaging.

"Packaging confirmed it. The glue seam pattern along the heat seal — it's a small thing, you'd miss it if you weren't looking — are consistent with the impurities. Two factories, two sealers, two operations running simultaneously."

He let that land for a second.

"This is a real professional operation, folks. They're not just cooking up batches in a basement. They're running factories, with quality control and everything. Which means we're not looking for one supplier to choke off. We need to find both."

The room murmured back at him.
The odds were stacked against them, already.
Now they had to take out two factories.

"Okay, next topic," he said, clicking to the next slide.
It showed a graph of recent crime statistics, with a sharp spike in robberies next to an image of the fallout from a recent bank heist.

"Bank robberies are on an all time high, and we need to be more vigilant in our patrols," he added.

Reed showed a new slide with recent robberies, pointing out areas of concern and patterns in the crimes.

Reed continued to go through the briefing and finished up with the usual assignments for the day.

"That's all for now, let's get to work," he said, dismissing the officers.

Everyone got up and started to leave the room, but Alex had noticed that he hadn't been given a specific task yet. So he waited for a moment, just in case.

And he was right.

Reed went over to Alex and pulled a chair out for him to sit on.

"Hey Murphy, how are you feeling?"

"Honestly, I'm not sure. It's been a lot to take in," Alex replied.

"I understand. It's a big change. So you want back into the saddle?"

"Yes, sir!"

Alex paused. "Sir, there's one more thing. The bombing — the investigation into who rigged my car. Would it be possible for me to sit down with the investigators?"

Reed looked at him for a long moment, then nodded. "Of course, Murphy. I'll set it up for you."

"Thank you, sir!"

Reed nodded and smiled.  "Well, I have a little surprise for you."

And with those words, the door opened and Anne walked in.

"Lewis!" Alex exclaimed, a smile spreading across his face.

"Hey Murphy, it's good to see you again," Anne said, returning the smile.

"Ready to roll with your old partner again?" Sergeant Reed asked.

"Absolutely," Alex replied without hesitation.

"Well then, your new car is ready, how about you take it for a spin and get back into the groove?" Sergeant Reed suggested.

"Sounds good to me," Alex said, eager to get back out on the streets.

Sergeant Reed threw the keys to Anne, but Alex intercepted them mid-air.

Anne popped a bubble of her gum and shook her head. "Some things never change, huh?" she said with a smirk.
