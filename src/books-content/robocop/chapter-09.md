Chapter 9 - Mental Therapy.

Dr. Eli Thorne pretended like this was going to be just another session, but that couldn't be further from the truth.

He checked his watch nervously. Any second now.

He started to hear a weird noise, like a thumping sound.

Thump, thump, getting louder, thump, thump. Then it stopped.

And even though he was expecting it, the loud knock on the door still made him jump a little.

The doctor cleared his throat. "Come in!"

He heard weird noises, something scratching at the door. Then the door handle lowered slowly, before the door was opened much slower than usual.

He had been sent a photo of RoboCop before the session, but seeing him in person was literally taking his breath away.

A massive human-like robot was slowly opening his office door.

A little bit of sweat was forming on Dr. Thorne's forehead.

Alex Murphy stepped inside the office, his heavy metallic footsteps cutting through the silence.

"I'm sorry for the loud knock, doctor, I am still learning how to control my strength."

"That's... that's quite alright, Alex," Dr. Thorne stammered.

Alex was followed by Kamari James, his physical therapist.

Dr. Thorne was impressed how calm Kam was. He acted like this was just another day at the office.

"Please, have a seat, Alex," Dr. Thorne said, pointing at a reinforced chair, that had been sent especially for Alex.

Alex sat down carefully.

"I see you are doing quite well, Alex," Dr. Thorne said, trying to sound casual. "What is it, a week now?"

"Yes, doctor. Kam has been helping me a lot," Alex replied, nodding at Kam.

"Not at all, it's all you," Kam said with a smile.

Kam verified that Alex was settled in. "Alright, Dr. Thorne, I'll let you two get started."

"Thank you, Kam," Dr. Thorne said, nodding at Kam and smiling.

Kam left the room, closing the door behind him.

Dr. Thorne took a deep breath, trying to calm his nerves.

He had read all the reports on Alex Murphy, but seeing him in person was an entirely different experience.

The sheer size and presence of the cyborg was overwhelming.

"If it's okay with you, Alex, I will start with just the most basic of questions," Dr. Thorne said, trying to keep his voice steady. "They might feel silly, but they will help to get the ball rolling, so to speak. Sounds good?"

Alex nodded. "Sounds good."

"How are you feeling today?"

Alex laughed softly, "I knew you would be asking that question, of course, and honestly, I tried to come up with a good answer, but I really don't know how to answer it yet. Things are just so surreal to me still."

"I completely understand, Alex. I can only imagine how overwhelming this must be for you."

Dr. Thorne paused for a moment, gathering his thoughts. "Look, let me be upfront with you, Alex. I don't think there is any point it talking around it. The question OCP is asking, is it the right thing to do, to save a human life, even if all that is left is the brain. The problem is, that you have not been given the chance to answer this question for yourself, before it happened to you."

Alex was impressed, he had expected some kind of small talk, but Dr. Thorne was going straight to the point.

This topic was in fact what Alex had been thinking about endlessly since the accident.

Dr. Thorne continued, "And I think the honest answer for you and for anybody is, we don't know yet."

Alex nodded. That was where he had arrived as well.

Dr. Thorne leaned forward slightly. "You are the first. You will be the case study for this kind of procedure and this just adds yet another layer of mental stress to what you are already going through."

Alex took a deep breath. He wanted to thank Dr. Thorne for nailing so well how he felt, but couldn't find the words.

Dr. Thorne continued, "So how about this, I won't sugarcoat it, there is a long road ahead of you. You and your family. There will be good days and there will be bad days. Some days you will feel like you are making progress, and other days you will feel like you are back at square one. But you have been given something here, that no one else ever has had before. You have been given a second chance at life, Alex, and maybe there is a pot of gold at the end of this rainbow. And my honest opinion is, that you should try your best to find out."

Alex looked at Dr. Thorne, "You're pretty good at this, doc."

Dr. Thorne smiled, "Thank you, Alex. That's very kind of you to say. I'm not gonna lie, I thought a lot about your situation, but I was nervous like hell today."

Dr. Thorne chuckled softly, "Seeing you walk through that door was... intimidating, to say the least."

Alex smiled. "I get that a lot."

Dr. Thorne took a deep breath. "Alright, Alex, here's the plan. We will take this one step at a time. We will focus on your mental health and well-being, and we will work together to help you adjust to your new reality. Sounds good?"

"Sounds good, Doc," Alex replied.

"So it seems like today, you are in a good mood, is that fair to say?"

"Yeah, I guess so. I mean, this morning was a bit rough, but after that, I was doing okay."

"So here is my offer, we talk how often and how many times as you want. OCP basically gave my department a blank check."

Dr. Thorne smiled. "So we can do this as often as you need. But in addition to that, should you ever feel like you need to talk, you can always reach out to me."

Dr. Thorne pushed a business card across the table. "Here is my direct line." He looked at Alex' body. "Do you have pockets?"

"Only one, but that's for my gun."

Dr. Thorne laughed nervously. "Alright, well..."

Alex held out his hand. Dr. Thorne placed the card into it.

Alex looked at it for a moment, then raised it slightly.

There was a soft click, barely audible, like a camera shutter.

"Got it," Alex said, setting the card back on the desk.

Dr. Thorne shook his head. "Incredible."
